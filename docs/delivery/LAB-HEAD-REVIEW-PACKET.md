# Lab attendance: review packet for lab heads

**For:** the lab heads of the pilot labs. **Not technical.** About 20 minutes to read. We need your honest judgement of whether this would work in your lab and whether you accept the risks below. Your answers become part of the design; **nothing here has been built yet.**

## What is proposed

1. A student books a time in your lab on the KNEST site. You (or your delegate) approve it, reject it with a reason, or suggest another time.
2. You assign an assistant to approved bookings. The assistant sees a list of the sessions they are covering.
3. On the day, the student opens the KNEST app on their phone and shows a code that **changes every 30 seconds**; the assistant scans it. The assistant's phone shows its own changing code; the student scans that. Both scans are sent to our server.
4. Attendance is counted as **verified** only when our server has received and checked both scans. If only one arrived, it is shown as **partly recorded**. If the phone had no signal, the scan is kept on the phone and sent later; until then it is shown as "saved on this phone, not yet verified".
5. If the camera or phone doesn't work, the assistant can record attendance **by hand**, with a reason. These are labelled "manual" and counted separately in reports.

## What it can and cannot prove

| It can | It cannot |
|---|---|
| Make a screenshot of a pass useless after about a minute | Prove the student is physically in the room. A friend could pass a live code to someone who is not there, within seconds. The assistant glancing at the student and their ID is what closes that gap, not the software. |
| Show who booked, who approved, who scanned, and when, with an audit trail | Work on a phone that has lost its saved keys (the student would need to sign in online once to be re-enrolled) |
| Keep working with no signal and catch up later | Guarantee that every phone, especially older iPhones, keeps unsent scans if the app is closed or storage runs out |
| Stop one person's phone being used to forge other people's attendance | Stop an assistant from forging their own side; manual entries and unusual patterns are therefore reported for you to review |

## Questions we need you to answer

1. Would a scan open a door, or only record that the student attended? (This changes how strict it must be.)
2. Would the two-way scan take too long at your door? Our target is under 15 seconds per student.
3. Who would be the assistants, and would they use their own phones?
4. What do you do today when someone does not turn up, or when equipment is damaged? Should attendance records support that?
5. Are you comfortable with manual entries being allowed, and with you reviewing them?
6. Which of the risks in the table above would you not accept?
7. What would make you stop using this?
